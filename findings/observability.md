# Observability field guide

This page is one of the digital takeaways and examples that support Productside's teaching and advisory services on the Agentic Development Lifecycle (ADLC). It is written so you can use it without opening the sources.

## What this phase is for

Observability is how a team knows what an agent is actually doing once real people and real systems are involved. It covers what you record, how you connect those records into a story of one task, what you measure over time, and how you notice that behavior has drifted or broken.

The decision it supports is a recurring one: **keep running, adjust, restrict, or stop**. Without evidence from operation, that decision falls back on anecdote and on whether anyone complained. For a product leader, this phase also decides who sees what, because the same telemetry serves engineers, cost owners, risk teams and regulators.

## What the sources agree on

1. **Healthy infrastructure does not mean a healthy agent.** An agent can return normal status codes while picking the wrong tool, looping, or giving a plausible wrong answer. Vendors and practitioners make this point repeatedly ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/monitoring-production-agent-lifecycle-with-aws-devops-agent-and-agentcore-evaluations/), [Mastra](https://mastra.ai/articles/ai-agent-observability), [Galileo](https://galileo.ai/blog/evaluating-ai-agentic-systems), [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/ai-agent-observability)); research on multi-agent failures supports the underlying pattern ([Cemri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)). *Fact* as a pattern; *Unknown* as a frequency.

2. **The trace is the basic unit.** One task is recorded as a trace made of nested steps (spans) covering model calls, tool calls, retries, handoffs, tokens and latency ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/concepts/trace-agent-concept), [OpenAI](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/), [Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-monitor), [OpenTelemetry](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)). Several sources add layers above it, such as a session or conversation, and below it, such as a single decision. *Fact* (this is how the documented tools work).

3. **Outcome checks are not enough; the path matters.** A right final answer can hide risky or wasteful steps, so teams are advised to inspect trajectories and the state the agent left behind ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/resources/agent-evals), [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/blog/2024-07-ai-agent-observability-with-langfuse)). *Inference*, consistently argued, little measured.

4. **Failures start early and compound.** Research on failed runs finds that an early error often cascades, and that the visible failing action is frequently not the cause ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370), [Cemri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)). A practitioner illustration is simple arithmetic: modest per-step error rates erode multi-step success quickly. *Fact* for the studied samples; *Inference* beyond them.

5. **Operational metrics must be joined by quality and cost metrics.** Recurring signals are latency, error rate, token use, cost per successful task, escalation to humans, sampled quality scores and user feedback ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/ai-agent-observability), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/ai-ml/guide/genaiops-for-mlops), [Institute of AI Product Management](https://www.institutepm.com/knowledge-hub/agentic-ai-metrics), [Microsoft on value](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agents/center-of-excellence/measure-report-value)). *Inference*; the lists overlap more than their thresholds do.

6. **Production traffic should feed the test set.** Sources describe one loop: sample live traces, review them, name failure types, add cases to the evaluation set, and keep scoring live traffic ([Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/field-guide/), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/how-to/traces-to-dataset), [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/resources/agent-evals), [Pollick](https://rickpollick.com/blog/evals-are-the-new-acceptance-criteria)). Reading real traces by hand before automating is recommended by practitioners and vendors alike. *Inference*.

7. **OpenTelemetry is the common instrumentation path, still settling.** Many vendors export in its format, so data can move between tools ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/concepts/trace-agent-concept), [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/observability-get-started.html), [CrewAI](https://docs.crewai.com/en/observability/tracing), [OpenTelemetry](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)). Microsoft and the standard itself say the agent conventions are still in development, and an analyst note calls the wider standards stack immature ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/agent-control-planes-still-need-a-robust-standards-stack/)). *Fact*, with a caveat on stability.

8. **Logs are also a privacy and security exposure.** Content capture is opt-in or opt-out depending on the tool, and defaults differ ([OpenAI](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agent-framework/workflows/observability), [CrewAI](https://docs.crewai.com/en/observability/tracing), [OpenTelemetry](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)). Trace access needs its own permissions ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-monitor)). *Fact*.

9. **Monitoring needs an owner who gets alerted.** An unwatched dashboard catches nothing; thresholds should come from your own baseline ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agents/center-of-excellence/measure-report-value), [Microsoft dashboard](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/how-to/how-to-monitor-agents-dashboard), [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/optimizing-production-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-observability/)). *Inference*.

10. **Regulation sets duties without specifying fields.** For high-risk systems in the EU, law requires event logging over the system's lifetime, deployer monitoring, provider post-market monitoring and serious-incident reporting on fixed deadlines ([Art. 12](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-12), [Art. 26](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26), [Art. 72](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-72), [Art. 73](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-73)). Government guidance elsewhere asks for continuous monitoring and auditing ([ACSC and partners](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/secure-design/artificial-intelligence/careful-adoption-of-agentic-ai-services), [Singapore IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/about/emerging-tech-and-research/artificial-intelligence/mgf-for-agentic-ai.pdf), [UK DSIT](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-cyber-security-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-for-the-cyber-security-of-ai)). *Fact* for the texts; whether a given agent is in scope needs counsel.

## Where they disagree or differ

- **Vocabulary.** "AgentOps", "agent observability", "LLMOps", "operability" and "control plane" overlap. IBM concedes AgentOps has no agreed definition ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agentops)); a research survey proposes a split between failures inside one agent and failures between agents ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01581)). One source separates observability (debugging real threads) from evaluation (scoring against a prepared set) ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-monitor)); others fold the two together ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/concepts/observability)).
- **Metrics versus error analysis.** Husain argues dashboards of generic scores give false comfort and that reading traces comes first ([Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/field-guide/)). Vendors lead with dashboards, alerts and automated scoring ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/how-to/how-to-monitor-agents-dashboard), [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/resources/agent-evals)). The two can be sequenced, but the emphasis differs.
- **Scoring scale.** Some favor pass/fail judgments ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/blog/2024-07-ai-agent-observability-with-langfuse), [Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/field-guide/)); others score 0 to 1 or 1 to 5 ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/concepts/evaluation-evaluators/agent-evaluators), [Mastra](https://mastra.ai/articles/ai-agent-observability)).
- **Sampling and thresholds.** Suggested live sampling ranges from scoring everything at first to roughly 10 percent ([LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/resources/agent-evals)); example alarms such as 95 percent run success or 5 percent error rate ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/how-to/how-to-monitor-agents-dashboard), [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/optimizing-production-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-observability/)) are illustrations, and the Institute's completion and escalation targets are likewise unvalidated ([Institute](https://www.institutepm.com/knowledge-hub/agentic-ai-metrics)).
- **Root-cause automation.** Vendors describe tools that diagnose and propose fixes ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/monitoring-production-agent-lifecycle-with-aws-devops-agent-and-agentcore-evaluations/), [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/observability-in-the-agentic-era)). Peer research finds automated failure attribution weak, with the best method naming the failing step in a small minority of cases ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)). Treat vendor claims as unproven.
- **How much to record.** A research proposal asks for scope proportional to risk because logging carries privacy costs ([Chan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13138)); the AgentOps taxonomy leans toward broad tracing across the whole lifecycle ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05285)).
- **Vendor slant.** Nearly every tool page treats its own platform as the answer. Statistics about how many firms lack observability are second-hand ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/observability-in-the-agentic-era), [Galileo](https://galileo.ai/blog/evaluating-ai-agentic-systems)). Consultancy papers promote governance services ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html), [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/ai/agentic-ai-governance-and-real-time-trust), [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2025/ai-governance-for-agentic-ai-era.pdf), [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-four-pillars-cios-can-use-to-scale-agentic-ai)).

## What to do in this phase

1. **Define what "working" means before launch.** Product, risk and engineering agree on task success, acceptable escalation, a cost per successful task and a latency budget per agent. Evidence: a one-page success definition with a baseline from the current process ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agents/center-of-excellence/measure-report-value), [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/optimizing-production-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-observability/)).
2. **Decide what to record, and what not to.** Choose trace content, retention, redaction and who may read traces. Involve legal and security. Evidence: a data-handling decision per field, including whether prompts and tool arguments are stored ([OpenAI](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/), [OpenTelemetry](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)).
3. **Instrument from day one.** Give every run a session identifier, step-level events, token and latency counts, an outcome label, and the agent and prompt version ([Uddin](https://dev.to/tamizuddin/from-hype-to-hard-reality-what-were-learning-about-shipping-ai-agents-in-production-42il), [Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-monitor)). Record version per turn, since a conversation can span versions.
4. **Read traces by hand.** On a regular cadence, sample real traces, take open notes, group them into failure types and count them. One builder reviews around 60 traces every week or two ([Torres](https://www.producttalk.org/ai-playbook/)). Evidence: a ranked failure list.
5. **Turn failures into checks.** Add each confirmed failure to the evaluation set, and score a sample of live traffic with the same checks ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/observability/how-to/traces-to-dataset)). Calibrate any automated judge against human labels.
6. **Set alerts with owners.** Pick a short scorecard, set thresholds from baseline, and name who is paged. Include cost spikes, loops, error types and policy violations ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/blog/2024-07-ai-agent-observability-with-langfuse), [Microsoft fleet](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/control-plane/monitoring-across-fleet)). Rehearse a failure in a non-production setting ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/optimizing-production-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-observability/)).
7. **Watch autonomy, not only errors.** Track override rates, approval patterns and agent-initiated stops to confirm human oversight is real ([IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/about/emerging-tech-and-research/artificial-intelligence/mgf-for-agentic-ai.pdf), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)). Add purpose-built monitoring for out-of-scope actions where agents have tool access ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing)).
8. **Run blameless reviews.** After incidents, write an impact statement, a timeline, contributing factors and owned actions ([Google SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/)). Check reporting duties if the system may be high-risk ([Art. 73](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-73)).
9. **Review on a rhythm.** Monthly, bring drift, cost, incidents and the failure list to the owner, and decide: continue, change, restrict or retire.

## Artifacts this phase should produce

| Artifact | Purpose | Minimum contents |
|---|---|---|
| Observability plan | Agree what is recorded and why | Signals per risk tier, retention, redaction, access, owners |
| Success and metrics scorecard | Shared definition of "working" | Task success, escalation, cost per success, latency budget, baselines, thresholds |
| Trace and logging standard | Consistent records across agents | Identifiers, version fields, naming, span types, outcome label |
| Alert and escalation map | Make signals actionable | Alert, threshold, named owner, first response, human fallback |
| Failure log and taxonomy | Learn from operation | Counted failure types, example traces, linked evaluation cases |
| Incident review | Prevent repeats | Impact, timeline, contributing factors, owned actions, reporting decision |
| Regulatory record map | Show duties and evidence | Applicable provisions, log retention, reporting contacts, counsel sign-off |

## Questions a product leader should ask

1. If this agent quietly got worse last week, what would tell us, and how soon?
2. Can we reconstruct one specific task end to end, including tool calls and the agent version?
3. What share of live traffic do we review or score, and who reads the results?
4. Which three failure types are most common, and how do we know?
5. Which alerts have a named owner and a defined first response?
6. What do our traces contain about customers, and who can read them?
7. What is our cost per successful task, and is it moving?
8. How often do humans override or stop the agent, and does that rate make sense?
9. When did a production failure last become a test case?
10. What triggers a serious-incident review, and who decides whether a regulator must hear of it?
11. What would we turn off or restrict if the evidence worsened?
12. Which of our thresholds came from our own baseline rather than a vendor example?

## Failure modes

- **Dashboard comfort.** Only uptime, latency and error rate are watched. *Early sign:* complaints arrive before any alert fires.
- **Logging everything, deciding nothing.** Data piles up with no review habit. *Early sign:* nobody can name the last decision made from a trace.
- **Unowned alerts.** *Early sign:* alert channels are muted or ignored.
- **Borrowed thresholds.** Vendor examples become targets. *Early sign:* thresholds are identical to the documentation's.
- **Sensitive data in traces.** Defaults capture prompts and tool arguments. *Early sign:* no one can say who may read stored content.
- **Judge trust without calibration.** Automated scores replace human review. *Early sign:* scores rise while user complaints do not fall.
- **Root-cause overconfidence.** Teams accept an automated diagnosis. *Early sign:* fixes target the visible action, not the upstream cause.
- **Averaging across task types.** *Early sign:* one healthy overall number hides a failing segment.
- **Silent drift from model or data changes.** *Early sign:* behavior shifts after a provider update with no version trail.
- **No link to retirement.** Evaluation sets and failure logs are lost at shutdown. *Early sign:* no plan for archiving them.

## Gaps in the evidence

This is a well-populated phase by count, yet the strength is uneven. Most guidance is vendor documentation or practitioner opinion; much of it describes features, not outcomes. Peer-reviewed or preprint work on agent failure exists but uses research-style tasks and 2024 to 2025 models ([Cemri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657), [Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370), [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)).

- **Unknown:** whether any monitoring approach measurably reduces incidents, cost or time to recover. No source shows controlled evidence.
- **Unknown:** defensible thresholds. All numbers here are rules of thumb.
- **Unverified:** the adoption statistics cited by vendors and consultancies, mostly second-hand.
- **Thin:** privacy, retention and redaction practice; most tool pages omit it. Retention periods beyond the EU deployer minimum are not covered.
- **Thin:** small-team guidance. Reference architectures assume large cloud estates.
- **Not covered:** agent-specific incident-response practice. The postmortem sources are for conventional services ([Google SRE](https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/)), and the incident registry describes public disclosures, not rates ([Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11030)).
- **Legal:** EU texts are summarized by the Commission's service desk as non-binding explanations; this guide is not legal advice.
- **Licenses:** none of the sources had a verified reuse license except one UK government publication under the Open Government Licence v3.0 as stated on its page.
