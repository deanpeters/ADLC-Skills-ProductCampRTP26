# Bibliography

This page is one of the digital takeaways that support Productside's teaching and advisory services on the Agentic Development Lifecycle (ADLC). It lists the books, courses and web sources that informed Productside's phase guides and working model. Each entry gives the author or publisher, title, date and link, with a short note in Productside's own words. These are reading suggestions: none of the source material is reproduced here. Verify dates and license terms at the original before reuse.

## Books and courses
Entries are in APA style, with a short note on what to read each for.

- Broda, E., & Broda, D. (2026). Agent factory: Building agents at scale. In *Agentic mesh* (chap. 14). O'Reilly Media.
  Read for: fleets, agent templates, fleet organization patterns, and operating, updating and retiring agents at scale.

- Kashaboina, M. (2026). The agentic AI development lifecycle. In *Practical multi-agent AI systems: How to architect, build, and scale next-generation AI systems that work in the real world* (appendix B). John Wiley & Sons.
  Read for: an eight-concern view of the lifecycle for multi-agent systems.

- Ozkaya, M. (2025). The Agent Development Lifecycle (ADLC): A 7-step guide [Video lesson]. In *Agentic AI architectures with patterns, frameworks and MCP*. Packt Publishing; O'Reilly Learning.
  Read for: a seven-step framing of the lifecycle, with emphasis on evaluation and agent operations.

## Lifecycle frameworks (IBM, Microsoft, adlc.io)

- Asaf Saar (adlc.io) (n.d.). [ADLC: Agentic Development Life Cycle (home and manifesto)](https://www.adlc.io/). A manifesto-style home page that presents ADLC as an operating model for software delivery in which agents do the executing and humans do the governing. It comes from a single practitioner, Asaf Saar, and is a position statement, not a tested standard.
- Asaf Saar (adlc.io) (2026-09-25). [Finding Your Way Into the Work (Chapter 7)](https://www.adlc.io/product-management/read/finding-your-way-into-the-work). The free-sample chapter of Asaf Saar's book on product management in the agentic era. It is a narrative, practice-oriented chapter about how a product manager starts building with AI agents and when to bring in other expertise.
- Asaf Saar (adlc.io) (2026-09-25). [Product Management in the Agentic Era (book landing page)](https://www.adlc.io/product-management). The sales and overview page for a digital-edition book by Asaf Saar on how product management changes when agents shrink the distance between an idea and a working product. It states the premise, themes, audience and relationship to the author's ADLC model.
- Asaf Saar (adlc.io) (2026-09-25). [Product Management in the Agentic Era: Table of Contents](https://www.adlc.io/product-management/read). The table of contents for Asaf Saar's book: 22 chapters in four parts, plus a preface, epilogue and back matter. It shows how the author organizes agentic product management around a loop.
- Asaf Saar (adlc.io) (2026-09-25). [Read a Sample: Finding Your Way Into the Work (Chapter 7)](https://www.adlc.io/product-management/sample). The sample-page version of Chapter 7 of Asaf Saar's book, offered as a free preview with a PDF download. The text is essentially the same as the chapter page, wrapped in a short sales header.
- IBM (n.d.). [IBM watsonx.governance](https://www.ibm.com/products/watsonx-governance). IBM's product page for watsonx.governance, a platform sold for governing AI systems and agents. It lists capabilities, analyst recognitions and headline results.
- IBM (2026-09-03). [New in IBM watsonx Orchestrate: Cross-platform agent discovery, custom evaluation and AgentOps Agent goes GA](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/new-in-ibm-watsonx-orchestrate-cross-platform-agent-discovery-custom-evaluation-and-agentops-agent-goes-ga). An IBM product announcement describing three generally available features in watsonx Orchestrate: discovery of agents built on other platforms, trace and custom-judge evaluation tools, and an agent that helps improve other agents.
- IBM (Amanda McGrath and Amanda Downie) (2026-06-23). [What Is Agent Lifecycle Management?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agent-lifecycle-management). An IBM explainer defining agent lifecycle management (ALM) as managing AI agents from planning and building through testing, deployment, monitoring, governance, optimization and decommissioning. It is one of the more complete vendor descriptions of a whole-life approach to agents.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What Is AI Agent Development?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-development). An introductory explainer from IBM's Think library that walks through the stages of creating an AI agent, from scoping to live monitoring. It is written for a general enterprise audience and sits alongside IBM's other topic pages on agents.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What is AI Agent Evaluation?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-evaluation). IBM's explainer on how to assess whether an agent does its job: what to measure, how to test, and how to iterate. It covers both text-producing agents and agents whose success is an action such as updating a record.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What is AI Agent Orchestration?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-orchestration). An IBM explainer describing how a network of specialized agents is coordinated to complete complex workflows, including the main orchestration styles and the steps to set one up.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What is AI Agent Security?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-security). An IBM overview of the threats that autonomous, tool-using agents face and the protections that reduce them. It is a general security primer rather than a technical specification.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What is an Agent Control Plane?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agent-control-plane). An IBM explainer defining the agent control plane: a central layer that deploys, operates, monitors and governs agents, while the agents themselves run in a separate data plane.
- IBM (IBM Think) (n.d.). [What is Multi-Agent Collaboration?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/multi-agent-collaboration). An IBM explainer on how several independent agents coordinate to handle tasks no single agent handles well, including collaboration strategies and example frameworks.
- IBM (IBM Think); author Dave Bergmann (n.d.). [What Is the Agent Development Lifecycle?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agent-development-lifecycle-adlc). IBM's definition of the agent development lifecycle as a structured, end-to-end method for building and managing enterprise agents, with six phases and practices for each. It is the closest seed to the ADLC concept itself.
- IBM (IBM Think); authors Gregg Lindemulder and Annie Badman (n.d.). [Why observability is essential for AI agents](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/ai-agent-observability). An IBM article arguing that agents need observability because their autonomy and reasoning make them hard to trace, and describing what telemetry to collect. It applies the metrics, events, logs and traces model to agent behavior.
- IBM (IBM Think); authors Molly Hayes and Amanda Downie (2026-04-28). [What is AI Agent Sprawl?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-sprawl). An IBM article defining agent sprawl, the uncontrolled spread of agents across an organization, and outlining how discovery, governance and standardization can contain it.
- IBM (Think insights) (2026-04-17). [Observability in the Agentic Era](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/observability-in-the-agentic-era). An IBM editorial article arguing that traditional observability cannot keep up with AI-powered systems and that agentic capabilities should be built into observability itself. It draws on survey figures and an IBM executive's comments.
- IBM (Think topics) (n.d.). [What are Agentic Workflows?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agentic-workflows). An IBM explainer that defines agentic workflows as AI-driven processes where agents decide, act and coordinate work with limited human intervention. It contrasts them with rule-based automation and lists their components.
- IBM (Think topics) (n.d.). [What is a Multi-Agent System?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/multiagent-system). An IBM explainer on multi-agent systems, in which several AI agents work together on tasks for a user or another system. It covers how they differ from single agents, how they are organized, and their benefits and risks.
- IBM (Think topics) (n.d.). [What Is Agentic Architecture?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agentic-architecture). An IBM explainer on the structure of agentic AI frameworks: the design that lets models plan, act, remember and reflect. It compares single-agent and multi-agent layouts and several internal agent designs.
- IBM (Think topics) (2026-02-27). [What is Agentic Engineering?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agentic-engineering). An IBM explainer on agentic engineering, the practice of using engineering expertise to direct and oversee AI agents that help build software. It positions the term as a more professional successor to "vibe coding".
- IBM (Think topics) (n.d.). [What is AgentOps?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agentops). An IBM explainer defining AgentOps as an emerging set of practices for managing the lifecycle of autonomous agents, building on DevOps and MLOps. It describes why agents need tracing and monitoring and summarizes IBM Research's approach.

- Amazon Web Services (n.d.). [Policy in Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/policy.html). AWS documentation for a feature that checks every agent tool call against written rules before the call is allowed. - Amazon Web Services (AWS Machine Learning Blog; four AWS authors) (2026-09-11). An AWS blog post that describes a two-layer monitoring design for a production multi-agent system, using a demo airline-booking scenario. - Amazon Web Services (AWS Machine Learning Blog; Joshua Lacy and Jenny Shen) (2026-07-31). [Optimizing production agents with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Observability](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/optimizing-production-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-observability/). Part 2 of an AWS how-to series on running agents in production. - Amazon Web Services (Mahsa Paknezhad, Ishan Singh, Shoaib Javed), AWS Machine Learning Blog (2026-09-08). [Automated agent evaluation with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore and GitHub Actions](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/automated-agent-evaluation-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-and-github-actions/). An AWS technical blog post showing how to gate changes to an agent on evaluation scores inside an automated release pipeline. It walks through a worked example in which a pull request is blocked when scores fall below a threshold.
- Amazon Web Services (Sehwag, Aminikhanghahi, Srinivasan, Singh, Gordley, Santos), AWS Machine Learning Blog (2026-03-31). [Build reliable AI agents with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Evaluations](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-reliable-ai-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-evaluations). An AWS announcement-style blog post introducing its agent evaluation service and explaining how to think about evaluating agents. It pairs product description with general practice advice.

[AgentOps: Operationalize agentic AI at scale with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/agentops-operationalize-agentic-ai-at-scale-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/). A long AWS blog post that frames "AgentOps" as DevOps adapted for agents, organized around four pillars and a lifecycle from planning to monitoring. [Build and deploy scalable AI agents with NVIDIA NeMo, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, and Strands Agents](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-and-deploy-scalable-ai-agents-with-nvidia-nemo-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-and-strands-agents/). [Equipping agents for the real world with Agent Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills). Anthropic's engineering introduction to Agent Skills: folders containing a main instruction file plus optional supporting files that an agent discovers and loads only when relevant. Product documentation from Anthropic on running its agent toolkit in production on infrastructure you control. Anthropic documentation on securing deployments of its coding agent and agent toolkit. - Anthropic (engineering blog) (2026-01-09). [Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). A long practitioner guide from Anthropic on how to evaluate agents that use tools over many turns. It defines terms, describes grader types, and lays out a step-by-step path from no evaluation to a maintained suite.
- Anthropic (engineering blog) (2025-09-29). [Effective context engineering for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents). An Anthropic engineering post that defines context engineering as curating what information a model sees at each step, treating it as the successor to prompt writing for agents. It covers prompts, tools, examples, retrieval and techniques for long tasks.
- Anthropic (Erik S. and Barry Zhang) (2024-12-19). [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). A widely referenced engineering essay from Anthropic that distinguishes workflows (fixed, code-directed paths through language-model calls) from agents (models directing their own steps with tools). It catalogs a small set of composable patterns and argues for simplicity.
- Anthropic (Jeremy Hadfield, Barry Zhang, Kenneth Lien, Florian Scholz, Jeremy Fox, Daniel Ford) (2025-06-13). [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). Anthropic's account of taking a lead-agent plus parallel-subagent research feature from prototype to production. It shares prompt lessons, an evaluation approach and operational problems.
- Anthropic (Lance Martin, Gabe Cemaj, Michael Cohen) (2026-04-08). [Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands](https://anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents). An Anthropic engineering write-up on the design of its hosted agent service. It explains how the team separated the model loop, the execution environment and the event log so each can fail or change independently.
- Anthropic (written by Justin Young) (2025-11-26). [Effective harnesses for long-running agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents). An Anthropic engineering post describing an experiment in getting an agent to make steady progress on a large build across many separate sessions, each starting without memory. - Chan, Wei, Huang and colleagues (arXiv preprint) (2025-01-17). [Infrastructure for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10114).
