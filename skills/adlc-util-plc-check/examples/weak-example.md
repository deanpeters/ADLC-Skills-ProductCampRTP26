# Weak Example: Product Lifecycle Check Anti-Pattern

Synthetic anti-pattern. All details are invented for teaching.

## Artifact

**Product:** Meal-kit subscription app. **Agent:** Onboarding agent.

Stage: Growth. The product is only two years old.

Fit: The agent has hit every activation target, so it fits.

Risk: None. Activation is up 12 percent this year.

Exit: Not needed; the product is growing.

Decision: Scale. Put the agent in front of every subscriber and give it authority to offer discounts, since a growing product needs more automation.

## Why It Passes

It names a stage, a fit, a risk, an exit, and a decision. The numbers are positive, the language matches the growth plan leadership wants, and the decision is bold.

## Why It Fails

- The stage is placed from the product's age, not from signals; flat subscriber growth and margin pressure point to maturity.
- Fit is judged by the agent's own launch measure, not by what the product needs now.
- Rising activation with flat subscribers is the stage risk itself, optimizing a decline, reported as success.
- "No exit needed" leaves the agent with no retirement trigger at all.
- Autonomy and reach are raised because the product is called growing, with no new evidence and no Stage 5 decision.
- The check decides strategy and grants new authority on the person's behalf instead of stopping at a gate.

## Repair

Place the stage from the direction of subscribers, margin, and competition, and mark it Inference. Write the question the product team owns now, and test the agent's job and measure against it. Name the stage risk and the evidence for it. Record a retirement trigger tied to the product. Stop at a decision; if the deal has changed, re-run the Agent Strategy Canvas before any expansion, and take any new authority to Stage 5.
