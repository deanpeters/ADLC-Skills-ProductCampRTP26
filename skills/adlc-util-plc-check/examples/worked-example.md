# Worked Example: Product Lifecycle Check

Synthetic example. All details are invented for teaching. The company, product, figures, and agent are fictional.

## Situation

An invented meal-kit subscription app launched two years ago. At launch, the team approved an onboarding agent that walks new subscribers through choosing a plan and their first three boxes, and answers their first questions. It was measured on activation: new subscribers who received a second box. It has run unchanged since. At the annual planning review, the Product Manager runs the check before leadership's request to "put the agent in front of every subscriber."

Invocation: `Use $adlc-util-plc-check on our onboarding agent. Here is the launch canvas and this year's subscriber and margin numbers.` Mode chosen: 2, Context dump.

## Product Lifecycle Check

### Product, Agent, And Deal

- Product: meal-kit subscription app.
- Agent: guides new subscribers through plan choice and their first three boxes.
- Approved deal: Agent Strategy Canvas, approved at launch, two years ago. Autonomy: act within limits for plan suggestions; handback to a person for refunds.
- Owner: growth Product Manager. Product strategy: the general manager.
- Date of this check: November.

### Stage Then And Now

| When | Stage | Signals | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| At approval | Introduction | Launch quarter; acquiring early subscribers | Fact |
| Now | Maturity | New-subscriber growth flat for three quarters; margin per box up; two competitors cut prices | Inference |

Has the stage changed? Yes.

### Stage Question

- Now: defend or reinvent? The general manager is choosing between defending the core subscription on retention and cost, and reinventing with a grocery add-on.
- The agent's work helps with neither. It is tuned for first-time subscribers, who are now a small share of customers.

### Stage Fit

| Part of the deal | Today | What this stage needs | Fit | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Onboard new subscribers | Retain existing subscribers, or test the add-on | Fitted an earlier stage | Inference |
| Autonomy level | Act within limits on plan suggestions | Unchanged until a new job has evidence | Fits | Fact |
| Success measure | Activation (second box) | Retention and cost to serve | Fitted an earlier stage | Fact |

### Stage Risk

- Biggest AI risk at maturity: optimizing a decline.
- Evidence: the team recently tuned the agent to push larger plans at signup, which lifted activation while overall subscribers stayed flat. Activation looks better and the business does not move.
- State: Inference.

### Exit

- Product end-of-life plan: none; the product is not in decline.
- Agent retirement trigger: none recorded. Assumption: the onboarding job should end or shrink if new-subscriber volume keeps falling.

### Decision

- Recommendation: Change the deal. Re-run the Agent Strategy Canvas for maturity before any expansion. Putting a launch-era agent in front of every subscriber would scale the wrong job. Run a Constraint Decay Check first, since the signup tuning changed what the agent is optimizing.
- Human decision: Change the deal, by the general manager, November. Expansion is parked until the new canvas is approved.
- Unresolved disagreements: marketing wants to keep activation as a measure.
- Next check: when the defend-or-reinvent decision is made, or next planning cycle.
- Hand-off: Constraint Decay Check, then the Agent Strategy Canvas, then Stage 1.
